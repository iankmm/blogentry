Date: 11-19-2025
Title: The Vast Silence
Category: thoughts
---
Does something excite you?

The question sounds simple. It sounds polite. It is the kind of thing you ask a friend over coffee when the conversation slows down. But when you strip away the niceties, it is a diagnostic test for the soul. It is the only metric that actually matters.

We are told that discipline drives us. We are told that duty and responsibility are what hold a life together. But that is a lie we tell ourselves to get through the boring days. The truth is that excitement is the only thing that makes time move forward. It is the gravity that pulls you out of bed. It is what makes the world look colorful. When you are excited about a person or a project or just a song you cannot get out of your head, you are alive. You are sending out a signal that says I am here and I want to be here.

But then the static sets in.

It does not happen all at once. An existential crisis is rarely a loud crash. It is a slow erosion. It is like watching the coast fall into the sea, inch by inch. You wake up one morning and realize that the things that used to make your heart race now do nothing for you. You look at the future. That vast open horizon used to thrill you. Now it just looks like time. It looks like endless time that you have to fill until you die.

That is the tragedy of losing the spark. It is not that you are sad. Sadness is a real feeling. It has weight. This is different. This is the absence of temperature. It is looking at a sunrise and knowing it is beautiful, but not feeling the warmth of it.

We forget that we are biological machines designed to hunt. We are designed to chase things. We need the want. Without the want, we are just ghosts in our own routines. We go to work. We pay bills. We smile at the cashier. We scroll through our phones. But the engine is off. There is no destination on the map.

You try to force it. You try to manufacture the feeling. You buy the plane ticket. You date the stranger. You start the hobby. You go through the motions of a person who is alive. You hope that muscle memory will trick your heart into beating faster. But excitement is the one thing you cannot fake. It is either there or it is not.

When it is not there, you are left with the heavy weight of your own awareness. You realize that you are standing in the center of a huge universe, and you are completely still.

So you stare at the ceiling in the dark. You search for a flicker. You search for anything that might make tomorrow feel different than today. You beg the universe to give you something to chase. You want something to hunger for. You want something you are afraid to lose. Pain is better than this numbness. Heartbreak is better than this flat line.

You close your eyes and ask the darkness the only question that can save you.

Again, does something excite you?
